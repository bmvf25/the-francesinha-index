How it was built

The project follows a simple, reproducible path from collection to presentation.

Workflow
  • 1. Collect
    Google Maps supplied restaurant ratings and review counts. Menu and service information used saved source links.
  • 2. Organise
    Google Sheets separated restaurants, opening hours, francesinhas and extras.
  • 3. Check
    IDs, prices, ratings, coordinates, categories and closed-hour rows were audited before analysis.
  • 4. Analyse
    Sheets handled the main calculations. Basic SQL queries used SELECT, WHERE, JOIN, GROUP BY, ORDER BY, COUNT and AVG.
A fairer rating

Why adjust Google ratings?

A rating based on 50 reviews is less stable than one based on thousands. The project therefore uses a cautious weighted value for ranking restaurants.

Adjusted rating = (reviews ÷ (reviews + 500)) × rating + (500 ÷ (reviews + 500)) × overall average

The 500-review value pulls small samples toward the overall average. More reviews give the restaurant’s own rating more weight. The original Google rating remains visible.

Services

Takeaway, delivery and reservations

The dataset records 28 confirmed takeaway restaurants, 22 confirmed delivery restaurants and 26 confirmed reservation restaurants. Unknown means “not confirmed”, not “does not exist”.

Opening hours

Sunday closes most often

Sunday had 17 closed restaurants and Monday had 11. The most common opening time was 12:00. The most common interval close was 15:00, often marking the end of lunch service.

Read the results carefully

A good comparison also says where its certainty ends.

What this project can show

It makes price, rating, location, category, fries, opening hours and services easier to compare in one place.

What it cannot prove

Google ratings belong to restaurants, not individual dishes. Prices and hours change. The fries comparison is observational and does not prove that frozen fries cause lower ratings.
